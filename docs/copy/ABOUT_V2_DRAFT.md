## The short version

CappingAlpha is a research site for people who bet on sports. It puts everything a handicapper or a professional bettor looks at before a pick in one place, and it keeps score on the people who post picks in public. Think of what a finance site does for an investor: the price, the history of the price, the numbers behind it, the news, and the track record of every analyst who called it. We do that for games and for cappers.

We do not sell picks, we do not rank picks, and nothing here is a recommendation. Records are what happened, not what will happen next.

## Who this is for

The sports fan who wants the details behind a game, the bettor who does their own work before a wager, and anyone who has ever wondered whether the person selling picks is actually any good. If you have opened three tabs to check a line, the injury report and what a capper's real record is, this site is the one tab.

## What you get on every game

Every game page is built the way an investor reads a ticker: the price, how it moved, what the market thinks, and the facts underneath.

- **Lines from the books.** Spread, moneyline and total from fourteen sportsbooks side by side, plus first-half and first-five-innings lines where they exist, player props and alternate lines, all refreshed through the day. A consensus line built from the books, anchored on the sharpest of them.
- **Line movement.** The opening line, every move we recorded, and the closing line. We have kept the closing line for every game and every book since July 2026; that archive is the one thing most odds services charge extra for.
- **What the market thinks.** Where the public money is (ticket share and money share), and the price on the prediction markets, Polymarket and Kalshi, next to the book price.
- **The facts under the game.** Injuries, probable pitchers, weather for outdoor games, venue and surface, each team's recent form and each key player's recent form and workload, head-to-head history.
- **Live.** Once a game starts: score, situation, win probability, play by play, leaders and team stats, and a live read on where the pregame prices stand against the game as it plays.
- **Who is on this game.** Every capper we track who has posted a pick on this game, on each side of each market, with their record, their money made and their ROI in that sport, the line and price they took, and when we recorded it. Counts are counts: more cappers on a side has not meant more wins in our data, so we show the number and nothing more.

## The capper database

This is the part no one else keeps. We follow the people who post picks in public, capture every pick we can see, and grade it ourselves against the final score at the line and price we recorded. Nobody self-reports here.

- **Who we follow.** Public handicapping pages and prediction markets: Action Network experts, Covers contest and consensus members, WagerTalk handicappers, bylined columns at CBS Sports, SportsbookWire, TheSpread, Sportsbook Review and Sports Betting Dime, and public bettors on Polymarket. We also follow a small number of community pick channels; people from those channels appear under a pseudonym unless they claim their profile.
- **What a profile shows.** Overall record, money made at a flat $10 a pick, ROI, and the same three numbers for every sport and every bet type they play, by month, with the full list of picks and the price on each one. A short bio for the public figures, written by us from their public work. Today's picks, live as they post them.
- **How the list is ordered.** By money made, with the sample size right beside it. A capper with fifteen NFL picks sits below one with sixty, whatever the fifteen did. ROI is always shown and always sortable, but money is the default, because ranking on ROI floats thin records with lucky longshots to the top.
- **How a capper goes live.** A capper appears on the site after their overall record clears a bar we set: a minimum number of graded picks and a minimum amount of money made. The current bar is shown on the Cappers page. Once a capper is live, they stay live, whatever the record does next. Before the bar, their picks are graded but not shown.
- **Streaks.** Five or more straight wins or losses show as a count next to the name. That is a count, not a forecast.

## Your own bets

Track a Bet records your wagers against the same lines and grades them the same way. A bet tracked before the start at a price inside the market's range is marked verified; a screenshot of a betslip can be read into a tracked bet without the image ever leaving your phone. Your record, your equity curve, your closing-line value once we have the close. Socials lets you follow friends, see their tracked bets, tail a bet into your own slip, and compare records on a leaderboard where only verified bets count.

## Tools

Free calculators for the math bettors do by hand: no-vig fair odds, expected value, parlay pricing, hold, hedge sizing, Kelly staking and bonus rollover. All of it runs in your browser.

## How we grade

Every pick is graded win, loss or push against the final score, at the line and the price we recorded when the pick appeared. Picks that reach us without a price are graded at -110 for sides and -115 for totals, and each record shows how much of it that applies to. In-game picks are never counted. A drawn soccer match grades a moneyline pick as a loss on both sides. Tennis matches that end early settle nothing. We re-check graded picks every few minutes and publish corrections.

Money on a record is a flat $10 on every pick at the recorded price. A win at +150 is +$15, a win at -120 is +$8.33, a loss is -$10. ROI is that money divided by the money risked.

Our record for a capper can disagree with the one they publish. Ours counts what we captured, which is not always everything they posted, and it is graded at the number we saw, which is not always the number they got. Every record surface on the site carries the same note: graded by CappingAlpha from picks we captured at the line we recorded, not the handicapper's own record, coverage may be partial.

## How we track prediction-market bettors

PENDING (the accuracy audit session writes this in plain English): a Polymarket wallet counts as a bettor when it takes one side of a game before the start and holds it; wallets that hedge, sell out or trade in and out are not counted, and positions opened after the start are never counted. The price on the record is the price the wallet paid, converted to American odds.

## Reading a record

A small number of picks says very little. A bettor with no edge at all lands anywhere from about 32% to 68% wins over 20 picks, and about 42% to 58% over 100. Records here carry the number of picks for that reason, and a "small sample" note under 50. Across everyone we grade, the spread of records looks a lot like what chance alone produces. We publish every record because that is what a record is for, not because a good one predicts the next one.

## Pseudonyms, corrections and removal

Community cappers are shown under a pseudonym. Anyone shown here can ask for a correction, a pseudonym, or removal from public view; every profile links to the form. We correct errors within ten days of a request and keep a public log of corrections. We never delete the underlying record; we change what is shown.

## What we are not

We are not a sportsbook. We do not take or place bets, and we do not sell picks. CappingAlpha is a sports information and data platform for adults. 18+. If betting stops being fun, the responsible gambling page has the numbers to call.
