How a pick earns its number

A pick's score is the credibility of the people standing behind it: 10 for an unknown name, up past 100 when ranked winners lead the way. This is the complete story of where that number comes from, in plain betting language.

Current as of July 29, 2026. What changed lately, newest first:
  • Jul 28Price gate: every graded bet logs its edge over the win rate its own odds required. 100 or more bets clearly below that bar pins a capper to the flat 10, whatever their rank (chapter 4c).
  • Jul 28Heavy favorites: moneyline golds at -300 or heavier stay on the board but are never tracked as bets, judged once at tracking time. The record was restated to the July 9 v4 launch (chapter 7).
  • Jul 28MLB tightened: points capped by each capper's own proven win rate, quality-weighted chip-ins, sport rank bonus off for baseball (chapter 3).
  • Jul 28WagerTalk added as a source. A CapperTek tracker shipped the same day and was removed within hours: that site reveals sides 30 minutes after start, and a pick we cannot read pregame is not a pick (chapter 2).
  • Jul 2477 v4-era MLB picks retired in the first restatement.
  • Jul 16The real conviction curve replaced the chunked reveal (chapter 6).
  • Jul 14T-60 line lock (the bet is priced 1 hour before start) and the pregame flip (the tracked bet follows whichever side leads until the true start) (chapter 7).
  • Jul 9The Wilson percentile engine: capper rank drives every point, the flat base is gone, sources no longer score, plus the break-even gate, the hard zero, and instant grading (chapter 4). The money gate followed July 13.
01

The big picture

The score answers one question: how much verified evidence stands behind this side of this game today? Not hype, not follower counts, not which chat room it appeared in. Evidence means graded results in our own ledger.

Three rules shape everything underneath:

RuleWhat it means
Credibility is earnedEvery capper, expert, wallet, and source starts near zero and earns points only through picks we graded ourselves. A famous handle walks in as a credible newcomer, never as a star.
Points are never subtractedNothing ever removes points from a pick. When a losing capper backs a side, the penalty lands as bonus points on the opposite side instead (chapter 3, fade points).
One scale everywhereThe picks list, the game popup, the detail page, and the history archive all show the same number for the same pick. 100 or more is gold. 75 to 99 is silver.
There is no base anymore. The old flat 45 that every pick started with is gone. It never carried information, and worse, it let weak picks sit closer to the tiers than they deserved. Now a pick from a nobody is worth 10, and everything above that has to be earned by the ranked people backing it. A number can only be big because somebody credible made it big.

02

Where picks come from

Five systems feed the board. All of them are free to run, and all of them are held to the same standard: we grade every pick ourselves against the final score. Nobody gets to bring their own record.

House rules for every source

Pregame only. A pick counts for the board only if the source's own timestamp is before the game started. Anything placed mid game is kept for the capper's record but never touches the board.

One person, one voice. If the same capper shows up on the same side of the same game from two systems, that is one pick with two receipts, not two picks. The systems can never stack points by echoing each other.

03

The score recipe

Six ingredients, all additive. The width of each bar shows how much of the total that ingredient can contribute at most. (An old seventh, a flat +5 for being a major sport, was retired on July 9: every listed sport got it, so it was five free points on essentially every pick.)

Best backer0 to 80

The engine. Every capper is ranked on one board by the Wilson score (chapter 4), and their percentile position decides what their picks are worth: the #1 capper delivers close to 80 points just by posting, a top 1 percent capper 70 to 80, a mid-pack capper around 15, a bottom-quarter capper nothing. The strongest ranked person on the pick sets this number. Even the very best name cannot reach gold alone; a gold needs corroboration.

A pick whose capper we cannot identify, or whose capper has no graded record yet, is worth a flat 10. Sources are not people: the Action Network pipe, the Discord rooms, the Polymarket firehose earn nothing as entities anymore. Only the individuals inside them do.

Backer stackno cap, tapered hard

What the other backers chip in. Each extra backer adds half of their own worth, scaled by how well they actually win: an elite record chips the full half, a break-even record chips almost nothing. Chips also shrink in pairs, so a crowd never snowballs. A backer needs 12 graded bets with us before their chip counts at all. MLB is stricter still: 20 graded MLB bets and a sharper quality curve (see The MLB rule below). Full detail in chapter 5.

Sport rank bonus0, 10, or 20

Specialists get extra. Each sport keeps its own Wilson ranking, and when the pick's best backer is that sport's #1 or top 5 percent, the pick gets +20. Top 25 percent gets +10. This one has no volume cap by design: a 5-0 tennis specialist sitting on top of the tennis board collects the full bonus. One exception: baseball no longer gets this bonus at all, because MLB picks are already priced from the MLB-only ladder and paying the same standing twice handed the top names 20 free points per pick (see The MLB rule below).

Market signals0 to 8

Three small, independent checks against real markets: prediction markets pricing this side higher than the sportsbook implies (moneylines only for now, up to 5), the betting line having moved toward this side since our first line snapshot (2), and 65 percent or more of public tickets sitting on the other side (1). Deliberately small caps for now. The full values are logged so their weights can be re-fit on evidence later.

Side lean0 to 5

The replacement for the old automatic home team bonus. Each night the system measures whether home or away sides have actually been more profitable per sport over the last 120 days (minimum 100 graded picks each way) and leans that direction. Tennis and golf are excluded because their home and away labels are just listing order.

Fade points0 to 8

When a capper on the official fade list (chapter 4) backs one side, the opposite side receives 3 to 8 bonus points. Scaled by how much we have seen that capper in this sport, so a losing MLB record fades MLB picks but says almost nothing about their rare NBA pick. If a proven capper is standing on the faded side, that side gets an offsetting bonus too. Nothing is ever taken away from anyone.

The totals gate. One extra check for over/under picks only: a pick cannot be tracked as gold if its best backer's own ledger on that sport's totals is in the red (no totals history at all passes; the gate only blocks demonstrated bleeding). Board totals have historically bled money, so gold totals require a backer who has not lost them.
The MLB rule (July 23, tightened July 28). Baseball gets its own, much stricter version of the first two ingredients, because the data demanded it twice.

Why. Over 21 days MLB golds went 77-83 while every other sport combined won over 63 percent. A good record in other sports simply did not carry into baseball, while the people with a real MLB track record were winning 60.5 percent.

MLB record or nothing. Baseball points come from the MLB-only ranking. Fewer than 20 graded MLB bets means the flat 10, no matter how good the tennis record looks.

Your win rate caps your points. Being the best of a weak pool is not enough; the cap follows the capper's own proven win rate:

Joiners chip by their own quality. Two proven 58 percent records agreeing land near 170, the strongest signal we track priced like it. A 55 percent record adds about 17, and a break-even record adds almost nothing, so elite agreement soars while a crowd of grinders stays silver.

No sport rank bonus for baseball. The ladder already prices MLB standing; paying it twice handed the top names 20 free points per pick.

What to expect. Gold baseball picks stay genuinely rare until cappers prove those records. The quality idea also spilled over everywhere: in every sport a joiner's chip scales with their own win rate (crowded WNBA golds were winning 52 percent while lightly backed ones won 65). Other sports can move onto the full rule if their numbers show the same pattern; the admin Cappers tab shows every sport's own ladder for exactly that call.

04

How a capper earns points

The moment a game goes final, the result lands on the permanent ledger of every backer who was on the pick, and the rankings recompute right then. The daily board reset never touches this ledger; a full sweep at 5:20am each night is the backstop. The ranking is one number with a long pedigree: the Wilson score, the same math Reddit uses to rank comments so that 900 upvotes out of 1,000 beats 3 out of 3.

1. The worst-case win rate

The Wilson score asks: given this record, what is the LOWEST the capper's true win rate could plausibly be? We use the cautious 99 percent version. A 7-0 heater has a great face but a thin file, so its worst case is modest. An 85-49 grind over 134 bets has a worst case that is genuinely strong, which is why a long grind can rank #1 overall while perfect little records sit below it. Volume is not a bonus; it is the proof that makes the number trustworthy.

The engine ranks everyone on the left edge of their band, the worst case. The big file's floor is higher, so it ranks higher. That is the whole trick.

2. One board, every capper

Everyone with at least one graded decision goes into a single ranking: Discord cappers, Action Network experts, Polymarket wallets, Covers contest players, WagerTalk pros, all treated identically. Your percentile position on that board maps to points through the ladder:

Points slide inside each band, so the top of the 5-15 band is worth 60 and the bottom of it 51. Higher rank always means more points. There is one deliberate cliff: the bottom of the top 1 percent band is 70, and the band below it starts at 67. Full influence is reserved for the very top of the board.

3. Thin records are capped, not banned

A hot 7-0 can rank high on the board, but what its picks can collect is capped until the sample earns trust: under 10 graded decisions caps at 50 points, 10 to 29 caps at 70, and 30 or more collects the full ladder value. Nobody golds a pick off a lucky week.

4. The break-even gate

Ranking high is evidence you exist; handing out points also requires evidence you win. The Wilson score rewards volume, so a capper with a long losing record can still out-rank a crowd of thin ones. The gate stops that from mattering: a capper's win rate (shrunk toward the coin flip on small samples, meaning a short record gets pulled toward 50 percent so a lucky 4-1 start cannot fake a proven winner) must clear 50 percent for their backing to be worth more than the unknown-name 10, and full value returns at 53 percent, roughly break-even at standard juice. Between the two it scales smoothly.

And below the gate sits the hard zero: a raw win rate of 49 percent or under contributes nothing. Not the 10, no chip-in, and no sport rank bonus even where that capper tops a sport board. A demonstrated loser is worth less than an unknown name; the flat 10 is reserved for records we have not seen yet, not records we have seen lose.

4b. The money gate (July 13)

Win rate ignores price. A capper who lives on heavy favorites can hit 61 percent and still lose money at flat stakes, because favorites pay little when they win and cost full price when they lose. So a second gate asks the ledger a blunt question: if you had bet one flat unit on every pick this capper ever posted, are you at breakeven or better? Small records are steadied the same way the win-rate gate steadies them, so a cold week cannot trip it.

Below breakeven their value slides down smoothly, and once the hole is deep enough (about minus five percent smoothed) they pin to the flat 10: no chip-ins onto other picks, no sport rank bonus, nothing above what an unknown name gets, until wins pull the ledger back up. The scale is deliberate. Being $40 down over 200 picks is normal variance and keeps most of its value, while a ledger down ten percent of everything it ever staked pins hard. Whichever gate is stricter, the win-rate one or the money one, is the one that binds. Rankings and bands stay untouched, and the fade rules stay on win rate; the money gate only controls what a capper's backing is worth. Release is automatic, since every graded slate recomputes the ledger.

4c. The price gate (July 28)

The money gate reads the ledger; this one reads the prices themselves. Every graded bet now logs its edge over price: how often the capper wins minus how often their own odds said they had to. So a capper living on huge favorites can post a 90 percent win rate and still be losing money, and the win-rate machinery can even rank them highly while it happens. We watched exactly that: one name went 19-2 and finished in the red.

When a capper has 100 or more graded bets and their smoothed edge over price sits at minus two percent or worse, the price gate fires: their backing pins to the flat 10 that an unknown name gets, no chip-ins, no sport bonus, until the edge recovers. Rankings and bands stay untouched, and thin records cannot trip it, because small samples are smoothed toward zero and zero is not clearly negative.

Sources are no longer cappers

The old system tracked each pipe (Action Network as a whole, each Discord room) as a pseudo-capper that could carry picks by itself. That ended on July 9: the Action Network pipe had quietly pooled enough volume to out-rank every human being we track, and it was minting golds off single unproven backers. Pipes deliver picks; only people earn points.

Consensus: the chip-in

The question consensus answers: when several tracked cappers land on the same side, how much extra is that agreement worth? The answer went through two designs in one day, and the second exists because the first failed in public.

The rule

The strongest backer counts in full. Every other backer chips in half of their own worth, scaled by their own proven win rate (July 28: a joiner whose shrunk win rate sits at 58 percent or better chips the full half, 54 percent chips about a quarter of it, break-even chips almost nothing), with two further brakes:

The pair taper. Within each ranking band, the first two extra backers chip in half their worth, the next two a quarter, the two after that an eighth. Agreement is worth something; an echo chamber is not worth an echo chamber's size.

The 12-bet floor. A backer needs 12 or more graded bets with us before their chip-in counts at all. 06

The reveal

Since July 16 the conviction curve is real. Every point lands on the public score at the moment it actually happened: a backer's points step the curve at the exact time their pick came in, and fade points tally the instant the opposite side draws fade-list action. The old chunked climb (show 25, sit, jump 9, then 12) is retired; what members watch IS the conviction building, not a dramatization of it.

What still stays hidden

Two layers of protection remain, and they are enough:

  • Bonus timingThe three formula-shaped components (sport rank bonus, market signals, side lean) each surface at one random moment per pick, always at least 3 hours before game start. A +6 market step at 11:23am cannot be matched to the line move that earned it. The moments are seeded per pick, so the board, the popup, and the curve always agree, and a moment never changes once dealt. A pick born inside the final 3 hours trickles them out within about 20 minutes of arriving, still finishing before start.
  • Backer nettingWhen a stronger backer overtakes, the old best backer folds into the stack at half value scaled by their own win rate, so the curve shows one combined step. A 45-point capper opens the pick at +45; an 80-point capper landing later shows one combined step of about +57 (their 80, plus the 45 folded in at roughly 22, minus the 45 already showing). Nobody can read a single capper's worth off a join.

The public number is simply the true score minus any bonus moments still ahead, so it always reaches the full true score before first pitch, tip, or kick.

Why it changed: the synthetic climb hid the real story and could drift from how the points actually accrued. The real replay is honest about timing where honesty is safe (who backed it, and when), and random only where the timing itself would leak the formula (the bonus components).

07

Gold, silver, and the record

TierLineTreatment
Gold100+The tracked tier. Saved permanently, graded publicly, and it is the record the site stands on. Totals must also pass the totals gate.
Silver75 to 99Styled on the board so strong picks stand out. Never tracked, never part of the public record.
Archive50+Quietly saved into the permanent pick history for research and capper grading. Not a public tier.

Gold is intentionally hard, and under the Wilson engine it takes a top name PLUS corroboration: the #1 capper alone is just under 80, so even the best record we track needs the sport rank bonus or real chip-ins from other ranked winners to cross 100. A crowd of strangers cannot do it, and neither can one name on its own. In practice that means a handful of golds on a full slate, every one led by a top-band capper with company.

The moment a bet is tracked

A pick becomes a tracked bet the instant its true score crosses 100 (a total also has to clear the totals gate). Not when it posts, not when the reveal finishes, not at game start. The whole life of a tracked bet:

Crosses 100bet recorded, filed under the day the game is played · totals need the totals gate · heavy check: an ML at -300 or worse stays board-only
T-60the CA line locks about 1 hour before start · the bet is priced here, wherever the score is
True startfirst pitch or first serve, not the listed time · score freezes, flips end
Finalgraded · every backer's ledger updated · rankings recompute on the spot
pregame: the score keeps climbing, and the tracked side can still flip to the leader

A pick can cross 100 before or after T-60. Earlier crossings track the market until the lock; later ones inherit the locked line. An overnight post about tomorrow's tennis files under tomorrow's record, not tonight's.

The bet is priced 1 hour before the game. The game's CA line locks within minutes of the 1-hour mark before the listed start (the old reach-gold lock is retired). Whatever the freshest book shows at that moment becomes the official line everywhere, and every tracked bet on that game is priced at it, as if the bet were placed right there. Grading and the P/L math use this frozen number no matter where the market moves afterward. A schedule change announced before the lock moves the mark with it; once locked, the line never re-opens.

The score can keep climbing; the price cannot move. Backers who arrive after the crossing still raise the recorded score, right up to the moment the game actually goes live. The listed start time is only an estimate (tennis especially runs late), so the freeze keys on the real first pitch or first serve, and the tracked score follows the board the whole way there. Once the game is live the score is frozen, and once a result is on the row, only a conflict void can ever touch it again: a graded win or loss never changes, but a later-discovered same-game conflict can still mark it void.

No price, no bet. A moneyline pick with no betting line available from any book is not tracked yet, because there is no P/L truth without a price. The row is written the moment a line shows up, and a pick whose line never materializes is never tracked. Spreads and totals settle at standard juice, so only moneylines wait.

Huge favorites do not get tracked (July 28). A moneyline gold priced -300 or heavier stays on the board and in the rankings, but no bet goes on the record. The math is unforgiving: at -1250 one loss erases twelve wins, and our own heavy-favorite bets went 41-14, winning 74 percent of the time, while losing almost seven units. The needed-win-rate chart in chapter 4c shows the trap: -300 demands 75 percent, and 74 is not 75. The check happens once, at the moment the pick would be tracked, using whatever the odds are right then. Tracked at -250 in the morning and drifting to -320 by evening rides; blocked at -320 and softening to -280 gets in on the next pass. The rule is scaffolding, not a belief about sports: the first capper who proves they beat heavy prices, 30 or more graded heavy-favorite bets with a positive edge over price, unlocks tracking for every heavy pick they back, automatically. The record was restated back to the v4 launch on July 9 under this rule, and the removed bets mostly won, which is the honest direction for a restatement: the shown win rate drops, the P/L stops paying juice it could never earn back.

One bet per direction, and it follows the leader. A game answers two separate questions: who covers the margin (moneyline and spread together) and where the total lands (over or under). Each can carry its own tracked bet, and they settle independently, so a tracked spread and a tracked over on one game are routine. Within a direction, if the other side overtakes the tracked pick while the game is still pregame, the bet flips: the old hypothetical bet is removed from the record (pending pregame bets are never public, so nothing visible disappears) and one flat unit goes on the new leader at the same locked 1-hour line. Same-team moneyline and spread are the same opinion, not a conflict, so both can ride. If both sides are dead even when the game goes live, both are voided (rare). After the start, nothing flips. For the future real-money mirror, a flip after a position is already open is the case that needs a hedge calculation on the prediction market side to cover the original stake; for the hypothetical P/L it is a clean swap.

The reveal has no say in any of this. Tracking keys on the true score, so a bet is often already recorded while members are still watching its public number climb.

A standing alarm watches the gold tier's trailing 30-day record. If gold ever stops beating the old system's benchmark, that alarm is the signal to recalibrate, and it is checked on real results, not vibes.

A day in the life

  • 12:30am ETThe pick cycle opens. Overnight posts count toward the coming day.
  • 4:58amFinal grading pass for late West Coast games, then the daily reset wipes the board. Permanent ledgers (capper history, pick history, gold record) are never touched.
  • 5:00amMorning build: game slate from ESPN, betting lines, the six pick slots per game, then a full re-scan of overnight messages.
  • 5:05amSource refresh: Action Network roster, Polymarket wallet list, Covers contestants.
  • 5:20amThe nightly re-rank. Every capper's Wilson score, board position, ladder points, sport ranks, and fade flags recompute from the full graded ledger. This is the full nightly sweep; during the day the same re-rank also fires the moment any batch of games grades, so results become scoring power right away.
  • All dayDiscord picks land instantly. Action Network polls every 10 minutes, Polymarket every 15, Covers and WagerTalk every 30. Every arrival rescores its pick slot and, if needed, starts a reveal.
  • 4:00pmAfternoon line refresh for evening games.
  • T-60: the line lockEach game's CA line locks 1 hour before it starts, and that is the moment the hypothetical bet is placed. Whatever the market shows then becomes the official line: the number the rankings display and every tracked bet is priced at and grades against. Before that a pick shows the current market line as a preview; once locked it never moves. A schedule change announced before the lock moves the mark with it; once locked, the line never re-opens. The tracked side can still flip to the other side of the game any time before the real start (at this same locked line); the score itself keeps tallying until the game actually goes live.
  • Every 5 minLive scores update and finished games get graded. Every backer on a graded pick gets the result on their ledger immediately, and the capper rankings recompute on the spot.

10

Glossary

ML (moneyline)
A bet on who wins the game, no point spread involved. Soccer moneylines have three outcomes; a draw grades both sides as losses.
Spread
A bet on the margin of victory. Brewers -1.5 means they must win by 2 or more.
O/U (over/under, totals)
A bet on combined points scored, over or under a line like 8.5. The line is the number; the juice is the price you pay to bet it (often -110 or -115).
Odds, American
-131 means risk 131 to win 100. +150 means risk 100 to win 150. Negative numbers are favorites.
Units
Profit measured in equal-sized bets at real odds. The site's honest currency for records: it accounts for whether wins came on favorites or underdogs.
ROI (return on investment)
Units won divided by units risked, as a percentage. A +8% ROI capper makes 8 cents per dollar bet over time.
Wilson score
The lower bound of the 99 percent confidence interval on a win rate: the worst the record could plausibly be. The site's one ranking number. Volume raises it; thin perfection does not.
Band
Where a capper's rank falls on the points ladder (top 1%, 1-5%, 5-15%, and so on). The band plus the position inside it decides what their picks are worth.
Volume cap
The lid on what a thin record can collect: under 10 graded decisions caps at 50 points, 10 to 29 at 70, 30 or more uncapped.
Best backer
The strongest ranked capper on a pick. Their ladder points, in full, are the pick's foundation.
Chip-in
What each additional backer adds: half of their own worth scaled by their own proven win rate, tapered in pairs per band, and only once they have 12 or more graded bets (baseball: a sharper quality curve and 20 graded MLB bets required).
Needed win rate
The win rate a price demands just to break even. -110 needs 52.4 percent, -300 needs 75, +150 needs 40. Shown as the Needed column on the Cappers tab.
Edge (over price)
A capper's actual win rate minus the needed rate their own average odds demanded. Positive edge tends to make money at any price; a clearly negative edge over 100+ bets trips the price gate.
Shrunk record (blend)
A capper's ROI after mixing in a skeptical baseline of imaginary break-even picks. Still used for the totals gate and fade routing details.
Source entity
A pipe (Action Network as a whole, a Discord room) formerly tracked as a pseudo-capper. Retired from scoring on July 9; shown in ops tooling only.
Fade
Betting against someone. A fade-active capper's picks add points to the opposite side of whatever they back.
Slot
One of six bettable sides per game the board pre-creates: home ML, away ML, home spread, away spread, over, under. Picks attach to slots.
Mention
One capper backing one slot, from any source. A slot's score recomputes on every new mention.
Reveal (formerly the leak rule)
Chapter 6. Backer and fade points show in real time; the three bonus components (sport rank, market signals, side lean) each surface at one seeded random moment, at least 3 hours before start. The public number is the true score minus moments still ahead. The old chunked climb is retired.
Conviction curve
The score-over-time chart on a pick's detail page. A real replay: every step sits at the actual timestamp its points arrived (bonus components at their reveal moments), and it always ends on the exact number the board shows.
Steam
The betting line moving toward a side since morning, usually meaning real money arrived on it.
Contrarian
Backing the side most public tickets are against. Historically the public loses slightly more than it wins.
Notional
The dollar size of a Polymarket trade. Each wallet's bet size versus their own usual is logged today, worth zero points until tested.
Grading
Marking a pick win, loss, or push from the final score. We grade everything ourselves; no source's claimed record is ever trusted.
